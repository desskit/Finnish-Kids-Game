## Unit 1: How Finnish sounds (Miltä suomi kuulostaa?) 👂

### 1. Read it like it's written _(explain)_

Finnish is one of the easiest languages in the world to read out loud. **Every letter always makes the same sound**, and you say every letter you see. No silent letters, like the k in English "knee"!

And you always press on the **first part** of a word: KIR-ja, PÖY-tä, O-pet-ta-ja. Say the rest lightly.


### 2. Double letters are long _(examples)_

When a letter is written **twice**, you hold that sound longer — a vowel *or* a consonant. That can change the whole word! Listen to all three:

- 🔥 **tuli** — fire — short u, one l
- 💨 **tuuli** — wind — long uu
- 🛂 **tulli** — customs (at the border) — long ll

### 3. Three new letters _(examples)_

- **ä** sounds like the a in "cat"
- **ö** sounds a bit like the u in "fur"
- **y** is "ee" said with round lips

- 👩 **äiti** — mom
- 🍽️ **pöytä** — table
- ✏️ **kynä** — pencil

### 4. What do you say back? _(pairs)_

Greetings come in **pairs**: someone says the first line, and you answer with the second. Tap 🔊 and say both out loud.

- 🗣️ **Hyvää huomenta!** — Good morning!  →  ↪️ **Hyvää huomenta!** — Good morning!
- 🗣️ **Mitä kuuluu?** — How are you?  →  ↪️ **Hyvää, kiitos!** — Good, thanks!
- 🗣️ **Kiitos!** — Thank you!  →  ↪️ **Ole hyvä!** — You're welcome!
- 🗣️ **Ole hyvä!** — Here you go!  →  ↪️ **Kiitos!** — Thank you!
- 🗣️ **Anteeksi!** — Sorry!  →  ↪️ **Ei se mitään.** — It's okay.
- 🗣️ **Näkemiin!** — Goodbye!  →  ↪️ **Hei hei!** — Bye bye!
- 🗣️ **Hyvää yötä!** — Good night!  →  ↪️ **Hyvää yötä!** — Good night!

### 5. Saying who you are _(pairs)_

When you meet someone new. Look at *nimesi* and *nimeni*: *nimi* means **name**, and the ending says whose — **-si** = your, **-ni** = my. (More about these endings in the "Whose?" unit.)

- 🗣️ **Mikä sinun nimesi on?** — What's your name?  →  ↪️ **Nimeni on Aino.** — My name is Aino.
- 🗣️ **Kuinka vanha olet?** — How old are you?  →  ↪️ **Olen seitsemänvuotias.** — I'm seven years old.
- 🗣️ **Hauska tutustua!** — Nice to meet you!  →  ↪️ **Niin sinuunkin!** — You too!
- 🗣️ **Kiitos ruoasta!** — Thanks for the food!  →  ↪️ **Ole hyvä!** — You're welcome!

**Q:** **Kuuntele!** Tap 🔊 — which word do you hear? Listen for the long sound.

- ✘ 🔥 **tuli** — fire
- ✔ 💨 **tuuli** — wind
- ✘ 🛂 **tulli** — customs

_Explain:_ You heard *tuuli*: the **uu** is held long. *tuli* is short all the way, and *tulli* holds the **l** instead.

### 7. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** Someone says *Hyvää yötä!* What do you say back?

- ✔ **Hyvää yötä!** — Good night!
- ✘ **Hyvää huomenta!** — Good morning!
- ✘ **Ei se mitään.** — It's okay.

_Explain:_ *Hyvää yötä!* (good night) is answered with the same words: *Hyvää yötä!*

### 8. Try it _(check)_

- ✘ a
- ✔ ä

_Explain:_ **ä** is the "cat" sound — like in *äiti*.

## Unit 2: No "a", no "the" (Ei "a" eikä "the") 👉

### 1. Two words Finnish doesn't need _(explain)_

English keeps putting little words in front of things: **a** book, **the** book.

Finnish has **no word for "a" or "the"** at all. *kirja* means "a book" or "the book" — you just say the word.


### 2. "This is…" _(examples)_

*Tämä on* means **this is**. *on* is a tiny word you will see everywhere — it means **is**.

- 📕 **Tämä on kirja.** — This is a book.
- 🧑‍🏫 **Tämä on opettaja.** — This is a teacher.
- 🎒 **Tämä on reppu.** — This is a backpack.

### 3. Asking with -ko _(examples)_

To ask a yes-or-no question, put *on* first and stick **-ko** on it: *Onko*. That's it — no "do" or "does" needed.

- 📕 **Onko tämä kirja?** — Is this a book?
- ✏️ **Onko tämä kynä?** — Is this a pencil?

### 4. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** Which one is a QUESTION?

- ✘ ⏰ **Tämä on kello.** — This is a clock.
- ✔ ⏰ **Onko tämä kello?** — Is this a clock?

_Explain:_ *Onko* at the start — with its **-ko** — turns it into a question.

### 5. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** How do you say "a teacher" in Finnish?

- ✘ a opettaja
- ✔ 🧑‍🏫 **opettaja** — teacher

_Explain:_ No "a" in Finnish — just *opettaja*.

## Unit 3: Counting (Laskeminen) 🔢

### 1. One to twelve _(examples)_

Eleven and twelve are "one-of-the-second-ten" and "two-of-the-second-ten": *yksitoista*, *kaksitoista*.

- 1️⃣ **yksi** — one
- 2️⃣ **kaksi** — two
- 3️⃣ **kolme** — three
- 4️⃣ **neljä** — four
- 5️⃣ **viisi** — five
- 6️⃣ **kuusi** — six
- 7️⃣ **seitsemän** — seven
- 8️⃣ **kahdeksan** — eight
- 9️⃣ **yhdeksän** — nine
- 🔟 **kymmenen** — ten
- 11 **yksitoista** — eleven
- 12 **kaksitoista** — twelve

### 2. One, or more than one? _(explain)_

With **one** thing, the word stays just as it is: *yksi kirja*.

With **two or more**, Finnish adds a little ending: **-a** or **-ä** (a few words take **-ta / -tä** instead). And the word stays singular — no "-s" like in English!


### 3. Spot the ending _(examples)_

- 📕 **yksi kirja** — one book
- 📕 **kaksi kirja[a]** — two books
- 🤝 **kolme kaveri[a]** — three friends
- 🖼️ **viisi kuva[a]** — five pictures

### 4. -a or -ä? _(examples)_

Look at the vowels in the word. Words with **a, o, u** take **-a**. Words with **ä, ö, y** take **-ä**. This "vowel team" rule comes back again and again in Finnish.

- 📕 **neljä kirja[a]** — four books
- ✏️ **neljä kynä[ä]** — four pencils

### 5. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** How do you say "two books"?

- ✘ 📕 **kaksi kirja**
- ✔ 📕 **kaksi kirja[a]** — two books

_Explain:_ Two or more → the word gets **-a**: *kirjaa*.

### 6. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** How do you say "three friends"?

- ✔ 🤝 **kolme kaveri[a]** — three friends
- ✘ 🤝 **kolme kaveri**

_Explain:_ Three is more than one → **-a**: *kaveria*.

## Unit 4: How to say "I have" (Minulla on) 🎒

### 1. There's no verb "to have" _(explain)_

In English you say *I have a bike*. Finnish has **no verb for "have"**!

Instead it says something like **"on me is a bike"**: *Minulla on pyörä*.


### 2. Who has it? _(explain)_

The **-lla / -llä** ending means "on". So:
- *minulla* — on me → **I have**
- *sinulla* — on you → **you have**
- *hänellä* — on him/her → **he/she has**
- *meillä* — on us → **we have**
- *teillä* — on you all → **you all have**
- *heillä* — on them → **they have**


### 3. Listen _(examples)_

- 🚲 **Minulla on pyörä.** — I have a bike.
- 📱 **Sinulla on puhelin.** — You have a phone.
- 🎸 **Hänellä on kitara.** — She/He has a guitar.
- 🐶 **Meillä on koira.** — We have a dog.
- 💻 **Heillä on tietokone.** — They have a computer.

### 4. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** *Sinulla on pallo.* — Who has the ball?

- ✘ I do
- ✔ You do
- ✘ We do

_Explain:_ *sinulla* = "on you" → **you** have the ball.

### 5. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** Which one means "we have a dog"?

- ✘ 🐶 **Minulla on koira.** — I have a dog.
- ✔ 🐶 **Meillä on koira.** — We have a dog.
- ✘ 🐶 **Heillä on koira.** — They have a dog.

_Explain:_ *meillä* = "on us" → **we** have.

## Unit 5: Saying you don't have it (Minulla ei ole) 🚫

### 1. "is" becomes "is not" _(explain)_

To say you **don't** have something, *on* (is) becomes **ei ole** (is not).


### 2. And the word changes too! _(examples)_

Here's the surprise: when you say you DON'T have something, the thing gets the **-a / -ä** ending — the same one you met after numbers.

- 👒 **Minulla on hattu.** — I have a hat.
- 👒 **Minulla ei ole hattu[a].** — I don't have a hat.
- 🧥 **Minulla on takki.** — I have a coat.
- 🧥 **Minulla ei ole takki[a].** — I don't have a coat.

### 3. Why? _(explain)_

Finnish uses **-a / -ä** when something is *not all there* — "none of it", "some of it", "more than one". You'll keep meeting this ending, so it's worth remembering!


### 4. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** Which one means "I don't have a shoe"?

- ✘ 👟 **Minulla ei ole kenkä.**
- ✔ 👟 **Minulla ei ole kenkä[ä].** — I don't have a shoe.

_Explain:_ After *ei ole*, the thing takes **-a / -ä**: *kenkää*.

### 5. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** *Minulla on hattu.* — Do I have a hat?

- ✔ Yes
- ✘ No

_Explain:_ *on* means "is" — so yes! For "no" it would be *ei ole* … *hattua*.

## Unit 8: Verbs tell you WHO (Kuka tekee?) 🏃

### 1. The end of the verb says who _(explain)_

In English: I eat, you eat, we eat — the verb hardly changes.

In Finnish, **the end of the verb tells you who is doing it**. That's why Finns often skip *minä* (I): *Syön.* already means "I eat".


### 2. Verbs come in families _(examples)_

Finnish verbs belong to **types** (families). Look at the END of the "to…" word to see which one:

- **Type 1** ends in two vowels: *laulaa*, *puhua*
- **Type 2** ends in **-da / -dä**: *syödä*
- **Type 3** ends in **-lla, -nna, -sta**: *tulla*, *mennä*

- 🎤 **laul[aa]** — to sing · type 1
- 🍽️ **syö[dä]** — to eat · type 2
- **tu[lla]** — to come · type 3

### 3. Each family makes "I…" its own way _(examples)_

- **Type 1**: drop the last letter → *laula-* + **n**
- **Type 2**: drop **-da / -dä** → *syö-* + **n**
- **Type 3**: drop **-la, -na, -ta** (or -lä, -nä, -tä), add **e** → *tule-* + **n**

*juosta* is a special one — learn it as it is: *juoksen*.

- 🎤 **minä laula[n]**
- 🍽️ **minä syö[n]**
- **minä tule[n]**
- 🏃 **minä juokse[n]**

### 4. The six endings — the same for every family _(explain)_

- **-n** → I
- **-t** → you
- (the last vowel doubles: *laulaa*, *tulee*; if it's already long, nothing changes: *syö*) → he / she
- **-mme** → we
- **-tte** → you all
- **-vat / -vät** → they


### 5. tulla — to come _(verbTable)_

The marked ending is the "who" part.

_tulla (come), present positive_

- minä tulen
- sinä tulet
- hän tulee
- me tulemme
- te tulette
- he tulevat

### 6. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** Which one means "we swim"?

- ✘ 🏊 **minä ui[n]**
- ✔ 🏊 **me ui[mme]**
- ✘ 🏊 **he ui[vat]**

_Explain:_ **-mme** means "we".

### 7. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** Which family is *mennä* (to go)?

- ✘ Type 1
- ✘ Type 2
- ✔ Type 3

_Explain:_ *mennä* ends in **-nna** — two consonants and a vowel: **type 3** (*menen*).

## Unit 0: When k, p and t change (K, P ja T vaihtuvat) 🔀

### 1. A letter in the MIDDLE can change _(explain)_

Some verbs change a sound in the middle, not just the ending. It happens to **k**, **p** and **t** — Finns call it **KPT**.

In type 1 the "to…" word has the STRONG sound (*nukkua*), and "I" gets the WEAK one (*nukun*): **kk → k**, **tt → t**, **pp → p**.


### 2. Strong → weak _(examples)_

The marked letters are the ones that change.

- 😴 nukkua → **minä nu[k]un** — I sleep
- 🧸 leikkiä → **minä lei[k]in** — I play
- ✍️ kirjoittaa → **minä kirjoi[t]an** — I write
- 🤝 auttaa → **minä au[t]an** — I help

### 3. He, she and they stay STRONG _(examples)_

Only **I, you, we, you all** get the weak sound. **He / she** and **they** keep the strong one: *minä nukun* but *hän nukkuu*.

- 😴 nukkua → **minä nu[k]un** — I sleep
- 😴 nukkua → **sinä nu[k]ut** — you sleep
- 😴 nukkua → **hän nu[kk]uu** — he/she sleeps
- 😴 nukkua → **he nu[kk]uvat** — they sleep

### 4. Other changes _(examples)_

**rt → rr**: *piirtää → piirrän*. And a **k** can disappear: *lukea → luen*.

- ✏️ piirtää → **minä pii[rr]än** — I draw
- 📖 lukea → **minä luen** — I read

### 5. Type 3 goes the other way round _(examples)_

In type 3 the "to…" word has the WEAK sound, and every person gets the STRONG one: *kuunnella → kuuntelen, kuuntelee*.

- 🎧 kuunnella → **minä kuu[nt]elen** — I listen
- 🎧 kuunnella → **hän kuu[nt]elee** — he/she listens

### 6. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** Which one means "I sleep"?

- ✔ 😴 nukkua → **minä nu[k]un** — I sleep
- ✘ 😴 **hän nukkuu**
- ✘ 😴 **sinä nuku[t]**

_Explain:_ "I" is **-n**, with the weak **k**: *nukun*. *nukkuu* is he/she — strong **kk**.

### 7. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** Which one means "she writes"?

- ✘ ✍️ **minä kirjoita[n]**
- ✔ ✍️ kirjoittaa → **hän kirjoi[tt]aa** — he/she writes
- ✘ ✍️ **me kirjoita[mme]**

_Explain:_ He / she keeps the strong **tt**: *kirjoittaa*.

## Unit 9: "Not" is a verb (En tee) ✋

### 1. A "no" word that changes _(explain)_

In English, "not" never changes. In Finnish, the **"not" word is a little verb** that changes for each person — and the main verb stays short.


### 2. syödä — not eating _(verbTable)_

The marked word is the "not" verb.

_syödä (eat), present negative_

- minä en syö
- sinä et syö
- hän ei syö
- me emme syö
- te ette syö
- he eivät syö

### 3. Same endings as before! _(explain)_

*en, et, ei, emme, ette, eivät* — look: **-n** for I, **-t** for you, **-mme** for we… the "who" endings moved onto the "not" word. The main verb loses its ending: *syön* → *en syö*.


### 4. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** Which one means "you don't sleep"?

- ✔ 😴 **sinä [et] nuku**
- ✘ 😴 **sinä nuku[t]**
- ✘ 😴 **hän [ei] nuku**

_Explain:_ *et* = "you … not" (the **-t** means you).

### 5. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** Which one means "they don't sing"?

- ✔ 🎤 **he [eivät] laula**
- ✘ 🎤 **he laula[vat]**
- ✘ 🎤 **me [emme] laula**

_Explain:_ *eivät* = "they … not", and the main verb stays short.

## Unit 12: Liking and loving (Pidän ja rakastan) ❤️

### 1. Every verb picks an ending _(explain)_

In Finnish, the **verb decides which ending** the next word gets. You just have to learn each verb's favorite.


### 2. pitää → -sta / -stä _(examples)_

"I like" is *pidän*, and the thing you like always gets **-sta / -stä** (it really means "from").

- ⚽ **Pidän jalkapallo[sta].** — I like football.
- 🍕 **Pidän pitsa[sta].** — I like pizza.
- 🎵 **Pidän musiiki[sta].** — I like music.
- 🍦 **Pidän jäätelö[stä].** — I like ice cream.

### 3. rakastaa → -a / -ä _(examples)_

"I love" is *rakastan*, and it always wants **-a / -ä** instead — or **-ta / -tä** after a long vowel, like *suklaata*.

- 🍕 **Rakastan pitsa[a].** — I love pizza.
- 🍫 **Rakastan suklaa[ta].** — I love chocolate.
- 🐱 **Rakastan kissa[a].** — I love the cat.

### 4. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** Which one means "I like music"?

- ✔ 🎵 **Pidän musiiki[sta].** — I like music.
- ✘ 🎵 **Pidän musiikki[a].**

_Explain:_ *pidän* always takes **-sta / -stä**: *musiikista*.

### 5. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** Which one means "I love pizza"?

- ✔ 🍕 **Rakastan pitsa[a].** — I love pizza.
- ✘ 🍕 **Rakastan pitsa[sta].**

_Explain:_ *rakastan* wants **-a**: *pitsaa*. (**-sta** is for *pidän*.)

## Unit 16: Seeing vs. watching (Näen ja odotan) 👀

### 1. The whole thing: -n _(examples)_

When you **see the whole thing** (just once), it gets **-n**.

- 🚌 **Näen bussi[n].** — I see the bus.
- 🚆 **Näen juna[n].** — I see the train.
- 🧑‍🏫 **Näen opettaja[n].** — I see the teacher.

### 2. Going on for a while: -a / -ä _(examples)_

*katsoa* (to watch) and *odottaa* (to wait for) take a while — so the thing always gets **-a / -ä**.

- 🎞️ **Katson elokuva[a].** — I watch the movie.
- 🚌 **Odotan bussi[a].** — I wait for the bus.
- 🤝 **Odotan kaveri[a].** — I wait for the friend.

### 3. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** Which one means "I'm waiting for the train"?

- ✘ 🚆 **Odotan juna[n].**
- ✔ 🚆 **Odotan juna[a].** — I wait for the train.

_Explain:_ Waiting goes on for a while → **-a**: *junaa*.

### 4. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** *Näen bussin.* — Which ending does "bus" have?

- ✔ -n
- ✘ -a
- ✘ -ssa

_Explain:_ Seeing the whole bus → **-n**: *bussin*.

## Unit 17: One whole thing, or some? (Kaupassa) 🛒

### 1. One whole thing: -n _(examples)_

Buying **one whole thing** — an apple, a banana — it gets **-n**.

- 🍎 **Ostan omena[n].** — I buy an apple.
- 🍌 **Ostan banaani[n].** — I buy a banana.

### 2. Some of something: -a / -ä _(examples)_

Milk, bread, juice — you don't buy "one milk", you buy **some**. Then it gets **-a / -ä**. (Remember: -a means "not all of it".)

- 🥛 **Ostan maito[a].** — I buy some milk.
- 🍞 **Ostan leipä[ä].** — I buy some bread.
- 🧃 **Ostan mehu[a].** — I buy some juice.

### 3. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** *Ostan maitoa.* — How much milk?

- ✔ Some milk
- ✘ One whole milk

_Explain:_ **-a** (*maitoa*) means "some of it".

### 4. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** *Ostan omenan.* — How many apples?

- ✔ One whole apple
- ✘ Some apple

_Explain:_ **-n** (*omenan*) means one whole thing.

## Unit 19: Describing words copy (Millainen?) 🎨

### 1. Colors _(examples)_

- 🟥 **punainen** — red
- 🟦 **sininen** — blue
- 🟨 **keltainen** — yellow
- 🟩 **vihreä** — green
- ⬛ **musta** — black
- ⬜ **valkoinen** — white
- 🟫 **ruskea** — brown

### 2. Describing words go first _(examples)_

Just like English: the describing word comes **before** the thing.

- 🐶 **iso koira** — a big dog
- 🏐 **punainen pallo** — a red ball

### 3. They copy the ending! _(examples)_

This is the cool part: whatever ending the thing gets, the **describing word gets it too** — like an echo.

- 🏠 **iso[ssa] talo[ssa]** — in a big house
- 🐶 **piene[n] koira[n]** — a small dog's
- 🍽️ **iso[lla] pöydä[llä]** — on a big table

**Q:** Which one means "in a big house"?

- ✘ 🏠 **iso talo[ssa]**
- ✔ 🏠 **iso[ssa] talo[ssa]**

_Explain:_ Both words get the ending: *isossa talossa*.

### 5. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** Which one means "of a red ball" (it takes -n)?

- ✔ 🏐 **punaise[n] pallo[n]**
- ✘ 🏐 **punainen pallo[n]**

_Explain:_ Both words take **-n**: *punaisen pallon*.

## Unit 20: Endings instead of "in" and "on" (Missä?) 📍

### 1. No word for "in" _(examples)_

English puts a little word **in front**: *in the box*. Finnish puts an **ending at the back**: **-ssa / -ssä** means "in".

- 📦 laatikko → **laatiko[ssa]** — in the box
- 🏠 talo → **talo[ssa]** — in the house
- 🏫 koulu → **koulu[ssa]** — at school

### 2. "On" is -lla / -llä _(examples)_

- 🍽️ pöytä → **pöydä[llä]** — on the table
- 🪑 tuoli → **tuoli[lla]** — on the chair
- 🛏️ sänky → **sängy[llä]** — on the bed

### 3. The vowel team again _(examples)_

Words with **a, o, u** → *-ssa, -lla*. Words with **ä, ö, y** → *-ssä, -llä*. Look at *talossa* vs. *metsässä*.

- 🏠 talo → **talo[ssa]** — in the house
- 🌲 metsä → **metsä[ssä]** — in the forest
- 🍽️ pöytä → **pöydä[llä]** — on the table

### 4. Look at the END _(explain)_

Sometimes the middle of a word changes a little too (*laatikko* → *laatikossa*). Don't worry — **the ending at the very end is what tells you "in" or "on"**.


### 5. In MY house _(examples)_

Remember the "my" ending **-ni**? It goes on the very end — **after** the place ending: *talo* + **-ssa** + **-ni** = *talossani*, "in my house".

- 🏠 talo → **talossa[ni]** — in my house
- 🚪 huone → **huoneessa[si]** — in your room
- 🍽️ pöytä → **pöydällä[ni]** — on my table

### 6. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** The cat is ON the table. Which is right?

- ✔ 🍽️ **Kissa on pöydä[llä].** — The cat is on the table.
- ✘ 🍽️ **Kissa on pöydä[ssä].**

_Explain:_ "On" → **-lla / -llä**: *pöydällä*.

### 7. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** The cat is IN the house. Which is right?

- ✔ 🏠 **Kissa on talo[ssa].** — The cat is in the house.
- ✘ 🏠 **Kissa on talo[lla].**

## Unit 21: In, into, out of (Missä? Mihin? Mistä?) 🚶

### 1. Three questions _(explain)_

- *Missä?* — where is it? (**in / on**)
- *Mihin?* — where is it going? (**into / onto**)
- *Mistä?* — where is it coming from? (**out of / off**)

Each question has its own endings.


### 2. Things you go INSIDE _(examples)_

- 📦 **Kissa on laatiko[ssa].** — The cat is in the box.
- 📦 **Kissa menee laatikk[oon].** — The cat goes into the box.
- 📦 **Kissa tulee laatiko[sta].** — The cat comes out of the box.

### 3. Things you go ON TOP of _(examples)_

- 🍽️ **Kissa on pöydä[llä].** — The cat is on the table.
- 🍽️ **Kissa menee pöydä[lle].** — The cat goes onto the table.
- 🍽️ **Kissa tulee pöydä[ltä].** — The cat comes off the table.

### 4. The pattern _(explain)_

**Inside:** -ssa (in) · -an, -oon, -seen… (into) · -sta (out of)

**On top:** -lla (on) · -lle (onto) · -lta (off)

Notice: **s** for inside, **l** for on top!

_Explain:_ "Out of" → **-sta / -stä**: *laatikosta*.

- ✘ 📦 **Kissa menee laatiko[sta].**

_Explain:_ "Into" → a long vowel + **n**: *laatikkoon*.

## Unit 23: Days and times (Milloin?) 📅

## Unit 10: Asking with -ko / -kö (Kysymykset) ❓

### 1. Any verb can ask _(explain)_

You know *Onko…?* — that is *on* + **-ko**. It works on ANY verb: take the "you" form and add **-ko** or **-kö**.

## Unit 24: Question words (Kysymyssanat) 🤔

### 1. The question words _(explain)_

- *Kuka?* — who?
- *Mikä?* — what? (what is it)
- *Mitä?* — what? (what are you doing)
- *Missä?* — where?
- *Mihin?* — where to?
- *Mistä?* — where from?
- *Milloin?* — when?
- *Montako?* — how many?
- *Kenen?* — whose?
- *Miksi?* — why?


### 2. The answer matches the question _(explain)_

Listen to the question word — it tells you which ending the answer needs:

- *Missä?* → **-ssa** (*laatikossa*)
- *Mihin?* → **into** (*puistoon*)
- *Mistä?* → **-sta** (*koulusta*)
- *Kenen?* → **-n** (*isän*)
- *Milloin?* → a time (*lauantaina*)


### 3. Ask and answer _(pairs)_

- 🗣️ **Kuka tuo on?** — Who is that?  →  ↪️ **Tuo on minun opettajani.** — That's my teacher.
- 🗣️ **Mitä sinä teet?** — What are you doing?  →  ↪️ **Luen kirjaa.** — I'm reading a book.
- 🗣️ **Missä kissa on?** — Where is the cat?  →  ↪️ **Kissa on laatikossa.** — The cat is in the box.
- 🗣️ **Mihin sinä menet?** — Where are you going?  →  ↪️ **Menen puistoon.** — I'm going to the park.
- 🗣️ **Milloin leikitään?** — When shall we play?  →  ↪️ **Lauantaina.** — On Saturday.

### 4. More questions _(pairs)_

- 🗣️ **Mikä tämä on?** — What is this?  →  ↪️ **Tämä on kirja.** — This is a book.
- 🗣️ **Mistä sinä tulet?** — Where are you coming from?  →  ↪️ **Tulen koulusta.** — I'm coming from school.
- 🗣️ **Montako kissaa sinulla on?** — How many cats do you have?  →  ↪️ **Kaksi.** — Two.
- 🗣️ **Kenen pallo tämä on?** — Whose ball is this?  →  ↪️ **Se on isän pallo.** — It's Dad's ball.
- 🗣️ **Miksi sinä itket?** — Why are you crying?  →  ↪️ **Koska olen surullinen.** — Because I'm sad.

- ✘ **Kissa on laatikossa.** — The cat is in the box.

## Unit 25: Me and you (Minua, minulle) 🫶

### 1. I, me, to me… _(explain)_

*minä* (I) changes its ending just like any word:

- *Auta minua!* — Help me!
- *Anna se minulle!* — Give it to me!
- *Pidän sinusta.* — I like you.


### 2. The endings you know _(examples)_

The same endings as the nouns: **-a**, **-lle**, **-sta**… plus one new one, **-t**, for "I see you".

- minä → **minu[a]** — me — help me, wait for me
- minä → **minu[lle]** — to me — give it to me
- sinä → **sinu[sta]** — you — I like you
- hän → **häne[t]** — him/her — I see him/her

### 3. Help me, help us… _(examples)_

- **Auta minu[a]!** — Help me!
- **Auta hän[tä]!** — Help him/her!
- **Auta mei[tä]!** — Help us!
- **Auta hei[tä]!** — Help them!

### 4. Which ending? _(explain)_

The verb decides, just like with nouns:

- *Auta, Odota* → **-a / -ä** (*minua, häntä*)
- *Anna se* → **-lle** (*minulle*)
- *Pidän* → **-sta / -stä** (*sinusta*)
- *Näen* → **-t** (*sinut, hänet*)


### 5. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** How do you say **"Help me!"**?

- ✔ **Auta minu[a]!** — Help me!
- ✘ **Auta minu[lle]!**
- ✘ **Auta minä!**

_Explain:_ *auttaa* takes **-a**: *Auta minua!*

### 6. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** How do you say **"Give it to him/her!"**?

- ✔ **Anna se häne[lle]!** — Give it to him/her!
- ✘ **Anna se hän[tä]!**
- ✘ **Anna se häne[stä]!**

_Explain:_ Giving TO someone → **-lle**: *hänelle*.

## Unit 14: Verb type 4 (Verbityyppi 4) 🔓

### 1. A new family: type 4 _(examples)_

Type 4 verbs end in a vowel + **-ta / -tä**: **-ata, -ota, -uta, -ätä**…

- 🔓 **av[ata]** — to open · type 4
- **hal[uta]** — to want · type 4
- 🧹 **siiv[ota]** — to clean · type 4

### 2. Drop the t — the vowels join up _(explain)_

Take off **-ta / -tä**, and put **a / ä** in its place: *avata → avaa-*, *haluta → halua-*. Then add the "who" ending: *avaan*, *haluan*.


### 3. avata — to open _(verbTable)_

The marked ending is the "who" part.

_avata (open), present positive_

- minä avaan
- sinä avaat
- hän avaa
- me avaamme
- te avaatte
- he avaavat

### 4. He / she: already long _(examples)_

The stem already ends in two vowels, so for **he / she** nothing more is added: *hän avaa*, *hän haluaa*.

- **minä halua[n]**
- **hän haluaa**
- 🧹 **minä siivoa[n]**
- ⏰ **minä herää[n]**

### 5. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** Which one means "I want"?

- ✔ **minä halua[n]**
- ✘ **hän haluaa**
- ✘ **sinä halua[t]**

## Unit 0: Type 4: the sound gets STRONGER (Vahvempi kirjain) 🦘

### 1. The other way round — like kuunnella _(explain)_

Remember *kuunnella → kuuntelen*? Type 4 works the same way: the "to…" word has the **weak** sound, and EVERY person gets the **strong** one: *hypätä → hyppään, hyppää*.


### 2. Weak → strong _(examples)_

**p → pp**, **k → kk**, **v → p**, **d → t**…

- 🦘 hypätä → **minä hy[pp]ään** — I jump
- ✂️ leikata → **minä lei[kk]aan** — I cut
- 🧗 kiivetä → **minä kii[p]eän** — I climb
- pudota → **minä pu[t]oan** — I fall
- tavata → **minä ta[p]aan** — I meet

### 3. Every person is strong _(examples)_

In type 4 there is no weak person form: *minä hyppään*, *hän hyppää*, *me hyppäämme*.

- 🦘 hypätä → **minä hy[pp]ään** — I jump
- 🦘 hypätä → **hän hy[pp]ää** — he/she jumps
- 🦘 hypätä → **me hy[pp]äämme** — we jump

### 4. Watch out: kiivetä _(explain)_

*kiivetä* ends in **-etä**, but it is a **type 4** verb all the same: *kiipeän*, like *hyppään*.


### 5. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** Which one means "I jump"?

- ✔ 🦘 hypätä → **minä hy[pp]ään** — I jump
- ✘ 🦘 **sinä hyppää[t]**
- ✘ 🦘 **hän hyppää**

_Explain:_ "I" is **-n**, with the strong **pp**: *hyppään*.

### 6. Try it _(check)_

**Q:** Listen! Which one did you hear?

🔊 plays: ✂️ leikata → **hän lei[kk]aa** — he/she cuts

- ✘ ✂️ **minä leikkaa[n]**
- ✔ ✂️ leikata → **hän lei[kk]aa** — he/she cuts
- ✘ ✂️ **me leikkaa[mme]**

_Explain:_ *hän leikkaa* — she cuts. The strong **kk** is in every person.

## Unit 0: Type 6: a sound gets stronger too (Tyyppi 6 ja KPT) 💨

### 1. Weak → strong, like type 4 _(examples)_

Type 6 verbs change the same way as type 4: weak in the "to…" word, strong in every person. **mm → mp**, and a **k** can even appear: *paeta → pakenen*.

- ♨️ lämmetä → **minä lä[mp]enen** — I warm up
- 💨 paeta → **minä pa[k]enen** — I run away

### 2. Every person is strong _(examples)_

- 💨 paeta → **minä pa[k]enen** — I run away
- 💨 paeta → **hän pa[k]enee** — he/she runs away
- ♨️ lämmetä → **hän lä[mp]enee** — he/she warms up

### 3. The whole picture _(examples)_

- **Type 1**: strong in the "to…" word, weak in *minä, sinä, me, te*.
- **Types 3, 4 and 6**: weak in the "to…" word, strong in every person.

- 😴 nukkua → **minä nu[k]un** — I sleep
- 🎧 kuunnella → **minä kuu[nt]elen** — I listen
- 🦘 hypätä → **minä hy[pp]ään** — I jump
