# Audit: lessons and "Why?" tips

A wrong rule, or a tip that doesn't match what the child actually picked, can
confuse a beginner more than no tip at all. This audit:

- read every lesson card;
- generated every "Why?" tip the games can show: every sentence frame with each
  wrong ending, every choose-the-form mode (real rounds from each course step),
  and samples from the verb, agreement, possessive, counting and
  find-the-mistake games;
- checked each one against the real sourced forms it describes.

## Rules that were wrong or incomplete (fixed)

| Where | Was | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Didn't" (Why tips, *Yesterday* lessons) | always **-nut / -nyt** | **-ut / -yt**: usually *-nut*, but *en tullut*, *en juossut*; plural **-eet** |
| Past tense | "slip in an **-i-**" | …and type 4 verbs get **-si-**: *halusin* |
| Present negative | "the main verb stays short" | the "I" form without its **-n**: *nukun → en nuku* |
| He / she ending | "last vowel doubles" | …unless it's already long: *syö* |
| Type 4 lesson | "for he / she nothing more is added: *hän haluaa*" (false: *halua → haluaa*) | the vowel doubles as usual, *haluaa*; only an already-long one stays, *avaa* |
| "Some" plurals (*palloja*) | **-ja / -jä** or **-ita / -itä** | also **-ia / -iä** (*koiria, kenkiä*), which was missing |
| Plural before *edessä* etc. | **-en / -jen** | **-jen, -ien, -iden…** (*veneiden*, *mehiläisten* were real forms) |
| "Into" | a long vowel + n, or -seen | …or **h** + vowel + **n**: *puuhun* (the course uses it) |
| Position words | "Before *alla*, the thing gets -n" (ambiguous) | "The thing gets **-n** and comes FIRST, then *alla*: *pöydän alla*" |
| *kanssa* | "**-lla** is only for tools and rides!" (overclaims: -lla also means on / at) | (**-lla** is for a thing you use: *pallolla*) |
| Ordinals lesson | "the first three are special" | *ensimmäinen* and *toinen* are special; from the 3rd on they end in **-s** (*kolmas*) |
| *paras* | told "**-in** means the most", then that *paras* is special | a special one gets its own explanation: *hyvä → parempi → paras* |

## Tips that didn't explain the WRONG PICK (fixed)

Choose-the-form games already said what each wrong option means (*Et juokse* =
"YOU don't run"). Seven other games only showed the general rule, whatever was
picked. Now each wrong pick says what that pick actually is:

- **Building a sentence / Review:** a wrong ending: *"laatikkoon is the INTO
  form. IN something → -ssa."* A wrong WORD (e.g. *laivalla* for *junalla*):
  *"the right form, but the wrong word. You need 'train'."* Before, it showed a
  grammar rule the child had already got right.
- **Verbs:** *"sinä nukut means 'you sleep'. The prompt is minä — 'I sleep'."*,
  then the ending rule. The k/p/t note shows only for verbs that change. A
  look-alike from another verb: *"juon is a different verb: 'I drink'."*
- **Describing words:** *"talolla is the ON form. But isossa is the IN form — the
  thing must copy it."*
- **My / your:** *"kirjasi means YOUR book. You need MY: -ni."*
- **Counting:** a wrong number → *"neljä is 4. Count again"*; a wrong thing →
  *"That's 'dog'"*. Before, both showed the -a ending rule.
- **Find the mistake:** separate tips for "all correct" when it wasn't, a word
  that can't be wrong, and a sentence that was already right.
- **Conversation scenes** had no tips at all. Now a wrong reply says what it
  means, and the deliberate wrong-form lines (*Kolme kynä.*, *Pidän pitsaa.*,
  *Menen bussiin.*) explain the slip.

## Other fixes

- **Pronoun tips** showed an unfilled blank: *hänelle* means "TO ___". Now
  "to him/her". The wording "the plain 'I / you / he' form" and "the -t ending
  (for 'I see you')" was also rewritten.
- **Owners:** pets no longer own pianos and coats ("The dog's piano"). They own
  a ball.
- **Comparing:** "younger than grandfather", "but mom is the oldest" → Grandpa,
  Mom.
- **Scenes:** wrong-form lines were glossed "(three pencil)" on the tile, which
  gave the answer away. They now show what the child meant to say. Also fixed:
  *hänen kirjansa* was glossed "their book"; it's "his book". The same
  mismatch was in the possessive game's English ("their cat" for the *hänen*
  forms), now "his/her".
- **Plural sentence frames** generated nonsense: *Nämä ovat englanteja* ("these
  are Englishes"), *Minulla on isiä* ("I have dads"), *Minulla on saaria*,
  *…poikia*, *…silmiä*. Uncountables, one-of-a-kind people and things, body
  parts and places are now kept out.

## New scenes

Ten units got a second scene, each aimed at the idea the first scene didn't
practise. Likes got a new scene in place of its old one.

| Unit | New scene | Drives home |
| --- | --- | --- |
| People | *Mikä tämä on?* | *Tämä on… / Onko tämä…?* |
| Numbers | *Montako?* | *yksi kirja* vs *kolme kynää* |
| I have | *Kenellä on?* | *meillä, hänellä, minulla* |
| Verbs 1 | *Mitä teette?* | who-endings, incl. *hän nukkuu* |
| Likes | *Mistä pidät?* (replaces the old scene, which never used *pidän*) | *pidän + -sta*, *rakastan + -a* |
| Shop | *Ostoslista* | *maitoa* (some) vs *omenan* (one) |
| Describing | *Punaiset pallot* | *kolme punaista palloa*: the echo |
| Where | *Missä tavarasi ovat?* | *repussani, pöydälläni* |
| When? | *Minun viikkoni* | *lauantaina* vs *aamulla, talvella* |
| Big numbers | *Karkkeja* | counting past twelve |
| Question words | *Kenen? Miksi?* | whose, how many, why, what |

All new and changed Finnish is flagged in `docs/FINNISH_REVIEW.md`.
