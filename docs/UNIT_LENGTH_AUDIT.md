# Audit: are the units long enough?

Finnish grammar is hard, and a unit that can be "finished" before the grammar
has really been practised gives a false sense of progress. This audit measured
every unit against how hard its grammar is, then lengthened the thin ones. Only
one unit was made smaller.

## How it was measured

For each unit:

- **Minimum practice.** The fewest questions a child needs to finish every
  step. A step is done only when its top level is *proven*: the adaptive engine
  needs 2 rounds at levels 1–2, then 3 or more rounds from level 3 up, all at
  85–95% right first time. A round is about 6 questions (a scene about 4, a
  story about 2–3).
- **Difficulty**, judged against what is hard for an English-speaking child:
  - **Hard:** case endings (places, objects, possessive and pronoun endings),
    the verb types and their consonant changes, the negative verb, plurals,
    the past tense, adjectives agreeing with nouns.
  - **Medium:** fixed patterns like *Minulla on*, *-ko / -kö* questions,
    commands, verb + verb.
  - **Light:** greetings and vocabulary units.

Real practice is always more than the minimum: every missed round makes the
climb longer. So the minimum is a floor, and the comparison between units is
what matters.

## The biggest finding: the ending was never actually tested

In the phrase steps, the answer tiles were different *words*, all already in
the right case (*kissasta / koirasta / pallosta*). The child picked the word,
and the ending came for free. The ending itself was only tested in the optional
Mestari unit, in Review, and in Find the mistake.

So **every phrase step now ends with a "Pick the ending" round** (level 3, and
the step's checkpoint question): the same word in several real forms, with only
one right for the sentence. There are 38 such steps. This changes how hard those
steps are more than the step counts below suggest. The wrong forms are only
ever forms that are wrong *for that meaning* (`content/contrasts.ts`), never
correct Finnish such as *Minulla on vettä* or *Olen koululla*.

## The one reduction: splitting the first verb unit

The old *Verbs, part 1* was the heaviest unit by far for a beginner. It taught
three verb families, then the consonant change (*nukkua → nukun*), in one
10-step unit with a single checkpoint at the end. It is now two units:

- **Verbs 1: types 1–3.** Plain verbs only, ending in a checkpoint, so the
  "who" endings are solid before anything else changes.
- **Verbs 1b: k, p and t change.** The consonant change, its own lesson (which
  used to sit part-way through unit 8), and its own listening step and story.

Nothing was removed; the content was divided, with consolidation in between.
Type 4 and types 5–6 (units 15 and 33) are just as long, but they come much
later, when the child already knows how verb units work, so they stay whole.

## Units considered and left as they are

- **Hello, Asking, Commands, Question words, Real conversations.** These are
  short, but their grammar is narrow and already practised through speaking
  and listening (greetings, *-ko / -kö*, commands, dialogues).
- **Describing (unit 20).** Agreement is hard, but the locative forms aren't
  known yet at that point. Agreement practice continues in unit 22 ("In the big
  house"), once the place endings have been taught.
- **The long verb units 15 and 33** (11 steps each). Verbs are the backbone;
  their part-way lessons already break them up.

## New kinds of practice behind these numbers

- **Kuuntele (listening), in 27 units.** The child hears a sentence and picks
  its meaning; the options differ only by the grammar (*laatikkoon / laatikossa
  / laatikosta*, *syön / syöt / hän syö*, *bussilla / bussiin*). The text stays
  hidden until a choice is made.
- **Which verb, which ending? (units 17 and 36).** The verbs are mixed, so the
  child has to read the verb first.
- **A story at the end of 32 units.** Read with the English first, then without
  it. Every word passes the real-word check against Wiktionary's forms.
- **Pick the ending** at the top of 38 phrase steps (see above).
